# Clipped Wing — Chapter 3: The Prince

Oct 4, 2026 · Harry Breeze-Lamb

Design for the third playable chapter, the prince's introduction. The wider cast and world notes live in [ideas.md](ideas.md). Chapter 2 is in [chapter-2-design.md](chapter-2-design.md).

## Pitch

An heir in one of the last human cities spends a week performing his duties: diplomacy and politics. His father shares his wisdom at times, and his diplomat (his "hand") is always at his side. Signs of civil unrest slowly build, and chaos erupts in the city below. By the end the prince realises the man at his side is the one behind it.

- **The overarching conflict of the game is introduced here,** but quietly. It comes to the forefront when the game returns to the capital (chapter 5, 6 or later).
- **The chapter ends with the prince still in the capital.**
- **The big decisions are big political decisions on the society:** the people below or high society above.
- **Design principle:** the choices set the state of the capital you return to.

## What the player knows

- The whole secret of the world and the tree is not told until chapter 4.
- Characters can allude to the problems and constraints they're under, but never actually say them. The trick is to make it seem like they're not holding anything back.
- The prince doesn't know what the symbol means.

## The world

- The society that's left is effectively only governance. It governs the other places, like the tree, and is the point of interaction between the integrated and the output of humans.
- It is sort of cruel: places like the tree are best managed unaware of their own situation. Humans have to remain useful.
- **The conflict:** the prince's father runs the steady ship. He carefully manages the relationship with the integrated. He's wise, knows disturbing it is a risk he won't take, and is tortured by the decisions he makes but believes this is the only way. The people trying to overthrow him aren't bad, just people striving to change society. Neither side is wrong.
- **The look:** quite futuristic, almost a futurist society, but with a castle vibe. It's high tech and trades with the integrated, so there are no old-fashioned roles like archivists.
- **Background only, never stated:** the integrated supply the capital's energy in exchange for human output, and send less when output drops. Unrest cuts output, which cuts energy, which feeds more unrest. The sealed wing hums when the father is negotiating, and nobody says with whom.

## How the unrest builds

- **In the low district** it's visible: the symbol spreads and people go quiet.
- **In upper society** it's felt but invisible: flat toasts, empty seats, polite allies. You feel the build but don't know why.
- **Midweek** you spot the symbol in upper society, stitched in your friend's sleeve.
- **The father** sits between both worlds.

## Locations

Height means status, as in the tree.

**The citadel (top)**
- **Your quarters:** private rooms with a balcony over the city. The tree is visible on the horizon.
- **The audience hall:** envoys and petitioners.
- **The council chamber:** politics, and empty seats.
- **Your father's study:** his wisdom.
- **The sealed wing:** a door only your father goes through. It hums, and nobody explains it.
- **The glasshouse:** the only greenery, where upper-society salons happen.
- **The banquet gallery:** dinners and balls, where you feel the tension.
- **The diplomat's office:** maps, letters, and visitors at odd hours.

**The city (below)**
- **The lifts:** glass lifts between levels, where messages change hands.
- **The data centre:** where the capital's requests and output come and go. Nobody says who's on the other end.
- **The promenade:** the mid-city market.
- **The low district:** cramped and full of the symbol.
- **The gate:** where envoys arrive from outside.

## Cast

**The citadel**
- **Your father:** the ruler. Wise and tired; the only one who goes through the sealed wing.
- **The diplomat (your hand):** warm and always at your side. Secretly spreading the symbol and connecting the plotters.
- **Your younger sister:** sharp and bored; sneaks where she shouldn't and notices things first.
- **The guard captain:** loyal to your father and suspicious of everyone. A red herring.

**Upper society** (tension felt, not seen)
- **The old councillor:** your father's oldest ally, now polite and distant. His seat keeps going empty.
- **The salon hostess:** runs the glasshouse and trades in gossip.
- **Your friend:** a noble heir your age. Midweek you spot the symbol stitched in his sleeve. The youngest plotter.
- **The envoy:** arrives at the gate from one of the places the capital manages. Nobody says what that means.

**The city** (unrest in the open)
- **The operator:** works in the data centre and notices things moving that shouldn't.
- **The lift operator:** sees who rides where, and when.
- **The painter:** a low-district kid who chalks the symbol and doesn't know who you are.

**The plotters** (each sympathetic, mostly only half-suspected this chapter)
- **The reformer:** a young councillor who argues openly for change. The obvious suspect, but a decoy the real plotters let you watch.
- **The commander:** runs the city watch, separate from your father's guard. Has quietly lost faith and is moving men into place.
- **The engineer:** keeps the lifts and citadel systems running. Knows what the sealed wing draws all that power for, and can't live with it.
- **The physician:** your father's doctor. Sees the decisions breaking him, and plots out of compassion.
- The diplomat connects them all.

## Big decisions

### 1. Energy (early)

The capital is short of power, and you decide who goes without.

- **Keep it with high society:** the citadel, the glasshouse and the gala stay lit, and the salons warm to you. The low district goes dark, and it's the low district that rises in the riot, led by a district firebrand.
- **Give it to the people:** lights, lifts and heat in the low district. The citadel dims, upper society sees a soft heir, and your father is quietly furious. The low district settles, but the promenade goes dark and the mid-city rises instead, led by **the reformer**.

### 2. The riot (middle)

A crowd turns violent. Who leads it depends on decision 1. Your hand gently pushes you to make an example of the leader, because he wants him inside the castle.

- **Bring him in to be executed:** high society approves. You believe he's executed, but he isn't, and you never find out this chapter. You've let a dangerous spy into the citadel: small things start going wrong inside, and nobody connects them to him. When you return to the capital, he's alive, leading, and knows the citadel from the inside.
  - If he's the district firebrand, the spy is a commoner who knows the streets.
  - If he's the reformer, bringing in a councillor for execution splits high society.
- **Let him go:** the people see mercy, but they also see your hand speak to the crowd and calm them. His hold on the crowd grows, and the leader stays a public face.

### 3. The broadcast (ending)

After the riot the city is waiting, and you address it on every screen. This decides where the diplomat ends up. Every path ends on the reveal that he's behind the disarray, with you still in the citadel.

- **Your father's script:** steady and calm. The city stays quiet on the surface, so the diplomat has to act in the open. He's exposed and flees the citadel. When you return, he's out in the city, against you.
- **The diplomat's draft:** warm and hopeful. The symbol goes out under your name, and only afterwards do you realise his words carried it. He stays at your side, trusted by everyone, and only you know. When you return, he's still your hand.
- **Your own words:** your father draws you closer and lets you feel the weight he carries, without telling you what it is. The diplomat keeps his distance. When you return, you're in your father's confidence.

## Medium decisions

- **Your friend's sleeve:** you spot the stitched symbol, not knowing what it means.
  - **Ask him about it:** he laughs it off but trusts you more.
  - **Mention it to the guard captain:** your friend starts being watched, and the captain trusts you.
- **Your sister at the sealed door:** you catch her trying to get through. She never gets in.
  - **Cover for her:** she becomes your eyes around the citadel.
  - **Tell your father:** he trusts you more, but she stops telling you things.
- **The envoy's plea:** a delegation from one of the managed places asks to send less. Nobody says what "less" is.
  - **Grant it:** you win their goodwill, but your father quietly corrects it behind you.
  - **Refuse it:** you're the steady ship, and they leave cold.
- **The physician's request:** he asks you to persuade your father to rest and hand more to you.
  - **Do it:** you take on more duties, which unknowingly helps the plotters.
  - **Refuse:** the physician turns distant.

## When the game returns to the capital

Chapter 5, 6 or later. The conflict about decision-making comes to the forefront. The ultimate choice is to reject or lead the revolution, which causes a massive split in the story. Chapter 3's choices decide who's where: whether the riot leader you thought was executed is out there leading, whether the diplomat is at your side or against you, and how close you are to your father.
