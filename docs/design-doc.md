# Clipped Wing — Design Doc

Sep 29, 2026 · Harry Breeze-Lamb

(Full export of the living design doc. The live version, with the interactive map diagram, is the source of truth.)

## Pitch

A short narrative RPG-mystery about a flawed woodcarver living inside a great tree, who starts a secret conversation with a stranger on the other side of the tube everyone worships.

- **Premise:** the tree people believe their offerings rise up a sacred tube to the Canopy, which returns blessings in exchange. The protagonist notices their returned carvings carry hidden messages, and replies.
- **Core loop:** carve, send, receive, decode. The relationship is built entirely through objects and a picture-symbol language.
- **Tone:** darkly comic on the surface (everyone is insufferably in awe of the Tree), bittersweet underneath.
- **Genre:** narrative RPG / mystery, with choices between loyalty and escape.
- **First release:** Act 1 only, 30–45 minutes, ending with the protagonist getting out of the tree. Expand later if it works.

## The tree

The tree is a meritocracy of craft: the better you make, the higher you live and the better your blessings.

- **Height = skill.** Master crafters live in the upper branches; the least skilled live in the roots, working rotting wood. Writing belongs to high society: roots-dwellers are never taught to read.
- **Offerings go up the tube.** A glowing, almost ethereal channel runs up the trunk. Everyone sends their best work up it at a morning rite. Blessings and rejections come down at an evening ceremony.
- **Offerings are ornaments.** The tree is a society of ornament makers; each family has its own kind of carved ornament. Anyone can carve a bird, but birds are the protagonist's family's craft. (Added 2026-09-30.)
- **Blessings come down.** Food, cloth, tools and light (glowing objects nobody in the tree could make). Everyone assumes these are divine. They are actually machine-made goods from outside.
- **Technology rises with height.** People believe a god gives them technological gifts. The upper branches are the most technological; the roots are mainly natural, with scavenged odd bits here and there that have made their way back down. (Added 2026-09-30.)
- **Rejection is unheard of.** No offering has been returned in living memory. A rejected piece is a divine verdict, so it is unclean: nobody will touch or open it, and it goes straight back to its shamed maker. This is the outsider's cover.
- **Fairness, brutally.** The tree sees itself as perfectly fair: you receive according to what you contribute. A brilliant carver makes better things, moves higher, and receives better materials, food, housing and status. It's a brutal meritocracy, and meritocracy assumes everyone has an equal chance to show their merit. The protagonist doesn't. (Added 2026-09-30.)
- **Devotion is total.** Everyone is insufferably in awe of the Tree. Doubt is unthinkable. Blessings have been shrinking for everyone lately, and people are hungry and scared.
- **The Ascension.** The tree's finest makers are chosen to give themselves to the tube, a sacred sacrifice everyone mourns and celebrates. In truth, they are captured and taken outside.
- **The judgement of skill is really outside taste.** What the tree believes is divine merit is what the Integrated find fashionable.

## The outside

Outside is a society so fused with technology it has lost the ability to make things, and it lives off the tree.

- **The Integrated.** A half-machine elite. Technology regulates their health, environment and economy. They don't perceive nature's value, so most of it is gone. Machine-made goods are cheap and meaningless to them; hand-crafted tree work is the ultimate status symbol.
- **Ordinary people.** Unaugmented, poorer, still dependent on nature. What's left of it is mostly the tree: fruit, sap and fibre for food, medicine and clothing.
- **Why it's desperate.** The tree has been overexploited for generations and is weakening. Ordinary people are starving. The Integrated don't care: the starving provide them nothing. They'll only notice when the crafts dry up.
- **The lever.** The tree's creativity is the one thing the Integrated value. If the tree stopped making, the elite would have to pay attention.

## The protagonist

A woodcarver whose missing thumb leaves the same flaw in every bird they make.

- **The accident (prologue).** As an over-eager child, carving before they were ready, a slip of the blade takes their left thumb and index finger. (Changed from the thumb alone, 2026-09-30.)
- **The flaw.** Every bird they carve carries the same mistake (for example, a heavy left wing). In the tree's eyes this makes them a lesser crafter.
- **The roots.** Their low standing puts them at the bottom of the tree, carving from the only wood they're allowed, which is rotting.
- **Why it matters.** The flaw that makes them worthless in the tree is what makes them findable from outside.
- **Home.** An only child who lives with their mum in the roots. When your blessings shrink she goes hungry too, and when you're shamed so is she.
- **Personality.** Naturally doubtful. Dialogue offers three ways of handling that doubt:
  - **Hide it:** perform devotion. Lowers suspicion, but people who could help never see the real you.
  - **Joke about it:** wry deflection. Charms some, unsettles others.
  - **Voice it:** blunt, sincere questions. Raises suspicion fast, but earns trust from the girl in the roots and the archivist.

## The outsider and the channel

The outsider, Grounded, is a stranger who went up the tube long ago. The interesting connection is why he chose your work, not who he is to you.

- **Who he is.** One of the Ascended, taken before you were born, so nobody in the tree remembers him. Now among the starving outside. Hidden in Act 1: the clues are that he knows the tree's customs a bit too well, and his markings are in the tree's style but with a precision no tree carver could manage, a hint of outside technology. Confirmed in Act 2, along with his role outside. His name, Grounded, is an Ascended promised flight who found bare ground; his symbol is a bird on bare ground.
- **His plan.** He wants you to become a martyr. If the cursed carver is publicly "accepted" by the tube, anything you later send back carries divine weight. The full plan is revealed in Act 2.
- **Why you (1): the flaw is a return address.** Among thousands of anonymous offerings, your birds always carry the same mistake. He can reply and know the same person will answer. Random messages would never build anything.
- **Why you (2): the rot is evidence.** Your roots wood shows the tree is sick. He may notice the rot first, then realise it's always the same maker.
- **How messages travel.** He rejects your bird, the first rejection in living memory. Because it's unclean, nobody opens it; it's handed straight back to you. Inside the hollowed bird is his message. Every message after that is another public rejection, costing you food and standing.
- **How you reply.** You learn to hollow your own birds without breaking them, awkwardly at first with the missing thumb. The exchange between a maker and the Tree is holy, so nobody inspects or handles an offering once it's given, and your replies go up safely.
- **The language.** You don't share a language. His messages are pictures because roots-dwellers can't read, a hollow bird only holds a tiny plate, pictures are deniable if found, and a devout person would never believe the whole truth at once. You decode each message yourself (see Gameplay). Your replies are picked from a few ready-made picture options at each reply point.

## Life in the tree

Most of Act 1 is managing daily life while the tree turns against you.

- **Daily cycle:** carve, send your offering, receive a blessing or rejection, then live with the fallout: neighbours, rites, errands, trouble.
- **Every message costs you.** Replying means another public rejection: less food and light for you and your mum, and more shame. The easy way out is always there: stop replying and life goes back to normal.
- **What stays secret:** the rejections are public; what's inside them is not.
- **The rules:** carve every day, attend the rites, speak of the Tree with reverence, stay at your station, never tamper with an offering. Breaking them, or just seeming odd, builds suspicion.
- **Scapegoat:** blessings are shrinking across the tree, and your rejections look like the reason. You become the explanation for everyone's hunger.
- **Escalation:**
  1. Whispers and gossip.
  2. People avoid you; your mum is snubbed.
  3. You're cut out of shared blessings.
  4. Your work is destroyed or your home vandalised.
  5. Nice, devout people start discussing killing you. They can't sacrifice you to the tube, because the Tree would only reject you.
- **High society:** gossip about the first-ever rejection travels up the tree. The archivist asks to meet you to confirm their suspicions. You can tell them the truth (a frightened ally with access and knowledge) or pander to the divine-rejection story (safer, but you lose them).
- **Shrinking blessings run through the whole act** and get visibly worse, clearly predating your first rejection:
  1. Ch 1: someone grumbles that portions are smaller.
  2. Ch 2–3: noticeably less; people start rationing.
  3. Ch 4: real hunger in the roots, while the upper branches stay comfortable.
  4. Ch 5: shortages everywhere and panic, which makes the scapegoating believable.
- **Jeopardy comes from lying or telling the truth** to the people around you: Mum, your friend, the Tender, the archivist. Every conversation risks exposing you or pushing someone away.

## Reveal ladder

The truth is revealed one curtain at a time, each feeling like a complete answer so the player never quite knows another layer is coming.

1. Something is odd about a returned bird. (Act 1, msg 1)
2. Someone is there, replying. (Act 1, msg 1)
3. There's an outside, beyond the bark. (Act 1, msg 2)
4. He's a person, and there are many of them; birds are real. (Act 1, msg 3)
5. They're starving, and you're not the cause. (Act 1, msg 5)
6. The Ascended are alive, hinted by the spiral. (Act 1, msg 6)
7. Grounded is one of the Ascended, and he has a plan for you. (Act 2)
8. The blessings are made by people, not the Canopy. (Act 2)
9. The full system: the Integrated, the starving ordinary people, and a dying tree holding both up. (Act 2+)

## Scope: Act 1

The first build is Act 1 only: a 30–45 minute standalone story that ends with the protagonist getting out of the tree.

- **Story span:** the prologue accident, life in the roots, the first-ever rejection, the growing correspondence, the tree turning on you, and the escape.
- **Big choices in Act 1:**
  1. Answer the first message, or ignore it (a short, comfortable loyal ending).
  2. With the archivist: tell the truth, or pander to the divine-rejection story.
  3. The ending: how you leave the tree.
- **The ending (branch and bottleneck):** the mob comes for you. Either way, you get out.
  - **Throw yourself into the tube.** Witnessed. The cursed carver is "accepted" by the Tree, so you become the martyr Grounded asked for, a sort of saint; the mob has to live with it; your mum is left with grief and honour. Act 1 ends as you rise.
  - **Cast down to rot.** You refuse to be anyone's martyr and are taken to the bottom of the roots, a prison of rotting wood. The tree thinks you died in disgrace; your mum is left with shame. In total darkness you push through rot so far gone it gives way, until natural light blasts in. Act 1 ends there.
- **Making choices feel real:** later characters refer to what you chose, the emotional aftermath differs, and small concrete differences (an item, a trusting person, an open door) carry forward.
- **Locations:** see The map.
- **Crafting:** a quick daily choice between a proper offering and a message bird, not a mini-game (see Gameplay).
- **Build:** a browser game in Phaser (TypeScript), with dialogue and story written in ink (inkjs), built with Vite, versioned on GitHub and hosted on itch.io. Built with Claude Code.
- **Expansion:** later acts cover the outside world, the Integrated, and the full reveal ladder.

## Act 1, day by day

Act 1 is a prologue and seven days, about 45 minutes. By day you survive; by night you solve the mystery. Each day has its own focus, and the week slides from belonging to being hunted.

**Prologue: The Slip**

- A child carving too early; a slip of the blade takes the left thumb and index finger.
- (2026-09-30) The reaction choice that introduces the three dialogue styles moves to Day 1 morning: Mum asks how you're feeling.

**Day 1: Ascension Day** (setup)

- Pure setup: talk to people, watch things unfold. On this day only, you can visit everyone, which teaches moving around the map.
- Mum is proud and devout; your friend dreams of being chosen; the Tender is busy with the rites; the girl in the roots watches from the edges; someone at the market grumbles that portions are smaller.
- **Evening:** the Ascension. A celebrated master is given to the tube; you notice their spiral.
- **Night:** the climb, to watch the Ascension lights.

**Day 2: The Rejection** (the last normal afternoon)

- **Afternoon (two visits from now on):** conversations in the three styles; rationing is announced gently; a small choice with your ration (keep it, or share some).
- **Evening:** your bird is rejected, the first time in living memory. **Your public reaction:** hide (bow your head), joke (a nervous quip) or voice ("Why?"). This sets your starting suspicion.
- **Home:** Mum wants it burned. Agree, stall or argue; either way she leaves it by the fire.
- **Night:** the fire, then the first decoding. **Big choice 1: reply or ignore.**

**Day 3: Your friend**

- **Morning:** you send the fledgling (or your usual bird).
- **Afternoon:** their family has forbidden you from coming near. The girl in the roots shows you a back route, and you sneak to their window. They promise to come to you tonight.
- **Evening:** message 2.
- **Night:** the visit. They arrive with message 2 still on your table; hide the evidence, then confide or lie.

**Day 4: The girl in the roots**

- **Morning:** you send your reply to message 2.
- **Afternoon:** Mum is snubbed at the market. Step in and it becomes a short real-time fight with a young zealot: dodge, shove, grab; you can't make a proper fist. Win and suspicion jumps; lose and you're hurt and lose food; hold back and Mum sees it. Then the girl, the one person who talks to you normally, says the rot is deeper than they say and asks you to come tonight.
- **Evening:** message 3 and the feather.
- **Night:** the rot, sneaking down to her home deep in the roots.

**Day 5: The archivist**

- **Morning:** you send your reply to message 3. Word spreads that someone from the upper branches is asking about you.
- **Afternoon:** the upper branches stop sharing with the roots; families are turned away. Mum gives you her portion, scrubs you clean and lends you her good cloak. One more visit of your choice.
- **Evening:** message 4 comes down; you decode it at home. An escort takes you up: your first sight of the upper branches' wealth. **Big choice 2: truth or pander.**
- **Night:** the archive (truth: a healthy seed) or the heist (pander: it fails, and you take a rotten roots seed without realising).

**Day 6: Scapegoat**

- **Morning:** your home has been vandalised and your carvings smashed. You send the seed with the pointing hands.
- **Afternoon:** using the girl's back routes, you tail the Tender to a meeting with the preacher and elders. They can't send you up the tube, because the Tree would reject you, so they discuss casting you down. Then the girl is punished for being kind to you, and you can't stop it.
- **Evening:** your bird comes back bitten. The crowd recoils, and you're seized for questioning.
- **Night:** the interrogation. Every lie has to hold. Released late, you decode message 5.

**Day 7: The Mob**

- **Morning:** you send your bird in a cage. The Tender takes it eagerly.
- **Afternoon:** you move as a ghost, sneaking by back routes because you're recognised everywhere. Two last visits: Mum; your friend (if things are intact); the girl (if you can reach her); the archivist (if an ally: a route or token for tonight).
- **Evening:** message 6. You decode it as the noise outside grows. Your friend bursts in to warn you (if you confided) or you glimpse them among the torches (if you lied).
- **Night:** **Big choice 3.** The tube: a chase up through the tree to jump in front of everyone. Or the roots: you refuse to be anyone's martyr and are cast down into the rot.

**The ending**

Act 1 ends on the threshold, with no epilogue.

- **Tube:** you jump, and Act 1 ends as you rise.
- **Roots:** in total darkness, you push through rot so far gone it gives way, until natural light blasts in. Act 1 ends there.

What happens to Mum, your friend, the girl and the archivist is left for Act 2.

**Tracked through the act:** suspicion, your friend's trust, the girl, the archivist (ally or not), Mum, Grounded's trust, the seed, and your dialogue style.

## Gameplay

Act 1 is a seven-day week in a Persona-style calendar: days for moving around the tree and managing relationships, nights for a different one-off set piece each time, and decoding Grounded's messages as the recurring puzzle.

**Each day**

- **Morning: the offering.** Choose what to send up: a proper offering (a chance at a blessing, food and standing) or a message bird for Grounded (guaranteed rejection and shame). Carving itself is a quick choice, not a mini-game.
- **Afternoon: free time.** Move around the tree and pick who to see. You can't visit everyone, so relationships, clues and suspicion build through your choices.
- **Evening: the descent.** Blessings and rejections come down in public, then home: Mum, a shrinking dinner, and decoding any message in secret.
- **Night: a set piece**, new every time, built for that moment in the story.

**The week**

| Day | Morning: you send | Evening: comes down | Night |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Your usual bird | A small blessing | The climb: platformer tutorial up the trunk to watch the Ascension lights; teaches movement and your weak grip |
| 2 | Your usual bird | Rejected: message 1 | The fire, then the first decoding: rescue the bird before Mum burns it, then crack it open alone |
| 3 | The fledgling (or your usual bird, the ignore path) | Message 2 | The visit: your friend drops by with the message still out; hide the evidence, then confide or lie |
| 4 | Your reply: yes, I can't, or why? | Message 3 and the feather | The rot: stealth through the rotting depths to the girl's home deep in the roots |
| 5 | Your reply to message 3 | Message 4: the seed request; the archivist summons you | The archive (ally: a search puzzle in the records, healthy seed) or the heist (no ally: stealth that fails, rotten roots seed) |
| 6 | The seed and the pointing hands | Message 5 in a bitten bird | The interrogation: the Tender and elders question you; keep every lie straight |
| 7 | Your bird in a cage | Message 6 | The escape: a chase up to the tube, or a crawl out through the rot |

**Decoding the messages**

- Each message is a row of pictures. Click a symbol and type what you think it means.
- Each symbol accepts a range of words (the eye: see, watch, look, notice).
- Wrong guesses get a nudge from your inner voice; hints strengthen after a few misses, so nobody gets stuck.
- Solved symbols go into a glossary in the player's own words, so later messages start half-decoded. Each message adds one or two new symbols.
- When every symbol is solved, your inner voice reads the whole line aloud.

**Other systems**

- **Keeping lies straight:** what you tell people is remembered; contradicting yourself raises suspicion.
- **Food at home:** a visible store that shrinks each night, showing the cost of your choices.

**Keeping it buildable:** two core engines, a side-view platformer (climb, rot, heist, escape) and a dialogue and choice system (conversations, the visit, the interrogation), plus the decoding screen and a few small one-offs.

**Inspirations:** Persona (calendar, time slots, relationships), Pentiment (small community, limited days), Firewatch (an unseen relationship through a limited channel), Chants of Sennaar (deciphering symbols), Papers, Please (daily ritual under a system), Night in the Woods (tone), Uncharted (a new set piece every chapter).

## The map

The tree is shown as a cross-section with two zoom levels: the whole cutaway as your map, and side-on scenes when you arrive somewhere. Most of Act 1 happens in the roots.

Layout, top to bottom: the Canopy (blinding light, unreachable); the upper branches (archive, upper halls, seed grove) with the Ascension gate on the tube; the trunk (trunk climb, middle tiers); the roots (friend's home, roots market, Tender's shrine, your home on the edge, the offering floor at the base of the tube); the back routes running beneath; and the lower roots at the bottom. The tube runs up the middle as a landmark from anywhere in the tree.

- **Height is status.** You live at the bottom near the rot, and everything you want is above you. The Day 5 escort and Day 7 chase climb the whole social order.
- **The roots are a tight hub around the offering floor**, which you return to every morning and evening.
- **Your home is on the edge, by the rot**: poor, forgotten, and close to the girl.
- **The back routes run beneath everything**, a hidden layer for the sneak days.
- **The Ascension gate gives the Day 7 chase its direction: up.**
- **Access:** the upper branches stay off-limits until Day 5; by Day 7 even familiar places are dangerous.

## Art direction

Clipped Wing is 2D, side-on pixel art at 480×270, scaled up crisply. It uses a tight palette built from the six key colours and their shades, with dithered light and glow. Detail lives in the backgrounds; anything that moves stays simple. (Changed from hand-painted ink and watercolour on 2026-09-30.)

**References:** Kingdom Two Crowns (side-on pixel art, dark and lit by glow), Owlboy and Eastward (detailed pixel worlds full of character), and Hyper Light Drifter (tight palettes, strong light). The earlier painted references, Planet of Lana (dark, readable figures) and Creaks (a gloomy but whimsical world seen side-on), still hold for mood.

**What gives it its own character**

- **A carved world.** Homes cut into living wood, chisel marks and grain everywhere, birds carved on shrines, railings and doorframes. The UI follows: dialogue on bark-like plates, and the decoding screen looks like the scratched plate itself. Ink linework reads naturally as carving.
- **Light tells you where you are.** Cyan is the tube (sacred, cold, not quite natural); amber is lamps and human warmth, richer the higher you go; green is the fungi of the lower roots (eerie, and secretly a sign of the rot). Climbing the tree means moving from green into gold.
- **Red is reserved.** Only the birds and you (a red thread on your character) are ever red. Red always means an offering or a message; the rising red birds are the signature image.
- **People belong to the wood.** Softly painted dark figures with slightly elongated, carved-figure proportions, lit from the side by the nearest light. Each is recognisable by one detail: your bandaged hand and red thread; the girl's glowing green strand; the Tender's amber crest of office; the archivist's pale ribbon of the upper branches.
- **The Canopy is pure white:** overexposed and featureless. The machinery at the top of the tree stays hidden in that light throughout Act 1.

**What's achievable**

- **Backgrounds carry the detail.** Painted scenes made with AI image tools guided by this style guide, cleaned up and split into a few layers (back, middle, front) for depth. Day and night are the same painting relit in code.
- **Life comes from code:** flickering lamps, drifting spores, fog, the shimmering tube and rising birds.
- **Characters are small and simple**, with short walk and climb cycles or cut-out (puppet) animation.
- **Portraits carry the emotion:** each main character painted with three or four expressions for conversations.
- **Rough volume for Act 1:** 12 areas (many one or two screens), a handful of animated characters, around 20 portrait expressions, the UI and the message plates.
- **First step:** one test scene of the lower roots with the tube, a lamp, fungi and your character, to prove the look before committing.
