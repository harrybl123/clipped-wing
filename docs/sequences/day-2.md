# Sequence plan: Day 2, The Rejection

A plan for the designer to mark up before it's built. **(doc)** = design doc; **(designer)** = decided
in conversation; **(suggestion)** = Claude's proposal. All dialogue below is a **first draft** for the
designer to rewrite; it keeps to the tree's faith and never hints at the truth.

## What the design doc asks for
- The last normal afternoon. Two visits from now on; conversations in the three styles; rationing is
  announced gently; a small choice with your ration (keep it, or share some). **(doc)**
- Evening: your bird is rejected, the first time in living memory. Your public reaction (hide: bow
  your head; joke: a nervous quip; voice: "Why?") sets your starting suspicion. **(doc)**
- Home: Mum wants it burned. **(doc)**
- Night: the fire, then the first decoding. Message 1: an eye, a clipped-wing bird, a clipped-wing
  fledgling: "I see you, clipped wing. Send me new life that can't fly." **(doc)**
- Big choice 1: reply (carve a fledgling) or ignore (your usual bird: rejected again with the same
  message; ignoring twice leads to the loyal ending, which ends the game early). **(doc)**

## Decided in conversation
- The day starts with the morning rite again. Someone in the crowd blurts out that they're hungry and
  tells people to buck their ideas up; the Tender shuts it down. **(designer)**
- Keep the separate afternoon conversations. **(designer)**
- The rejection comes at the evening ceremony, once you've spoken to some people, and it's dramatic.
  **(designer)**
- The crowd shuns you; you and Mum get away; she wants you to get rid of the bird. **(designer)**
- Mum asks *you* to burn it. You're about to, when you notice it's been tampered with, and it rattles
  when you shake it. You rescue it. **(designer)**
- Then an interactive decoding, then the first big choice. **(designer)** The word game should take
  inspiration from other games to be engaging. **(designer)**

## The shape of the day (suggestion)

| Time | Where | What happens | Objective shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Morning | Home → offering floor | Mum sends you off; the rite; the hungry outburst, shut down by the Tender, who then gently announces smaller shares; your usual bird goes up | "Take your bird to the morning rite" |
| Afternoon | Roots (two visits) | Pick two: your friend; the ration hatch at the market (keep or share your ration); Mum; the man from the outburst | "Spend the afternoon (2 visits left)" |
| Evening | Offering floor | Blessings come down. Your bird comes back: rejected. Your reaction. The crowd pulls away; Mum takes you home | "Go to the evening ceremony" |
| Night | Home | Mum: burn it. At the hearth you notice a seam you never cut; it rattles. You open it: a tiny plate. The decoding. The big choice | "Burn the bird" → "Open it" |

The areas held back on Day 1 stay held back on Day 2 **(suggestion)**; the lower roots and back routes
open on Days 3 and 4 with the girl.

## Draft dialogue

### 1. Morning (home)
Card: *Day 2.*
> **Mum:** Up you get. Same as every day: the rite first, then whatever the day brings.

Objective: *Fetch your bird from your bench* → *Take your bird to the morning rite.*

### 2. The morning rite (offering floor)
The crowd; the Tender on the landing. As you arrive, a thin man pushes forward.
> **Thin man:** Offerings! Look at us. Half a bowl a day, and we're still sending our best up the tube.
> **Thin man:** Somebody up there needs to buck their ideas up!
> *(The crowd goes still.)*
> **Tender:** Enough. *(The staff comes down on the landing.)* The Tree sees every hand that gives, and every mouth that grumbles.
> **Tender:** Hunger is a test. The Tree has never failed us. It will not start now.
> *(The thin man shrinks back into the crowd.)*
> **Tender:** *(gentler, to everyone)* Until the blessings are fuller, the hatch will share out a little less. So that everyone has something.

Then you give your bird:
> **Tender:** The carver from the edge. Same as ever. *(it goes in the basket; the tube brightens)*

Time moves on to the afternoon. Objective: *Spend the afternoon (2 visits left).*

### 3. Afternoon: two visits (pick any two)
**Your friend** (friend's home):
> **Friend:** Did you hear that man this morning? In front of the Tender!
> - *Hide:* **You:** He shouldn't have said it. Not there.
> - *Joke:* **You:** He's got a point about the bowls, though.
> - *Voice:* **You:** Is he wrong? People are hungry.
>
> **Friend:** *(hide)* That's what I said. *(joke)* Don't, someone'll hear you. *(voice)* ...The Tree knows what it's doing. It always has.

**The ration hatch** (roots market):
> **Hatch keeper:** One share. Less than yesterday, I know. Don't look at me like that.
> *(Outside, a small child from the lower roots watches your bundle.)*
> - *[Keep it]* Mum will need it.
> - *[Share some]* You break off a piece and hold it out. The child snatches it and runs.

(Keeping it adds to the food store; sharing gives less, and is remembered.)

**Mum** (home):
> **Mum:** A man shouting at the rite. In front of the Tender. What's the tree coming to?
> **Mum:** Keep your head down, love. The Tree looks after those who look after it.

**The thin man** (offering floor, alone now):
> **Thin man:** Go on. Tell me I'm wrong. *(he won't look at you)* My girl hasn't eaten since yesterday.

After two visits: *Go to the evening ceremony.*

### 4. Evening: the rejection (offering floor)
The crowd; blessings come down into the baskets. The Tender calls out each family.
> **Tender:** A blessing, for the weavers. A blessing, for the potters. *(and so on)*
> *(Then the tube goes dark for a moment. Something drops into the basin with a hard knock.)*
> *(Your bird. Untouched.)*
> **Tender:** *(very quietly)* ...Rejected.
> *(Nobody moves. Nobody has ever seen this.)*
> - *Hide (bow your head):* You bow your head and say nothing.
> - *Joke (a nervous quip):* **You:** Maybe it just... fell?
> - *Voice:* **You:** Why?
>
> *(Suspicion: hide +0, joke +1, voice +2.)*
> **Tender:** The Tree has spoken. Take it. It is not ours to touch.
> *(People step back from you. A whisper: "Cursed." Someone pulls a child away.)*
> **Mum:** *(taking your arm)* Come. Now. Don't look at anyone.

The scene travels home.

### 5. Night: the fire (home)
> **Mum:** It can't stay in this house. Burn it. Tonight.
> - *Hide:* **You:** You're right. I'll do it now.
> - *Joke:* **You:** Waste of good wood.
> - *Voice:* **You:** It's my bird. Why should I burn it?
>
> **Mum:** *(hide)* Good. *(joke)* Don't. Not tonight. *(voice)* Because the Tree said no. That's why.
> **Mum:** I'm going to bed. It had better be gone by morning.

Objective: *Burn the bird* (marker on the hearth).
> *(At the hearth. You hold it over the flames. Then you stop.)*
> *(There's a seam along its belly. A cut you never made. When you shake it, something rattles inside.)*
> - *[Burn it]* Your hand won't do it. *(it loops back: the choice is only to open it; or, if the designer wants, burning it is the "ignore" path from the start)*
> - *[Open it]*
>
> *(You work the seam open with your knife. Inside: a thin plate, no bigger than your thumbnail was, scratched with tiny pictures.)*

### 6. The decoding (see below)
When it's solved:
> **You** *(inner voice)*: I see you, clipped wing. Send me new life that can't fly.

### 7. The big choice
> *(Tomorrow's offering. What will you carve?)*
> - *[A fledgling with a clipped wing]* (reply)
> - *[Your usual bird]* (ignore: rejected again tomorrow with the same message; ignore twice and it's
>   the loyal ending)

Sleep ends the day.

## The decoding game (suggestion)

The design doc's version: click a symbol, type what you think it means; each symbol accepts a range
of words; nudges after wrong guesses; hints strengthen so nobody gets stuck; solved symbols go into a
glossary in your own words; when every symbol is solved, your inner voice reads the line aloud.

To make it engaging, borrowing from games that do this well:

1. **Find the pictures by turning the plate in the firelight** (like carving in the prologue, and
   *Heaven's Vault*'s handling of objects). The scratches are faint; turning the plate towards the
   fire makes each symbol catch the light. You only see one clearly at a time, which makes each one
   a small discovery.
2. **Warmer / colder feedback on guesses** (from word games like *Contexto*): a wrong guess still
   tells you something. "eye" for the eye symbol is warm ("close: what does an eye *do*?"); "tree"
   is cold. Each symbol has a small list of near words as well as right ones, so it's cheap to build.
3. **Hints from your own memories** (in the spirit of *Chants of Sennaar*, where meaning comes from
   context you've seen): after a couple of misses, your inner voice recalls something from the story.
   For the eye: Mum in the prologue, *"Everything we send up, it sees."* For the clipped wing: the
   Tender, *"Same bird as ever."* Hints are diegetic, and they reward paying attention.
4. **The line assembles in your own words** (the doc's glossary): each solved symbol drops your word
   into a sentence strip at the bottom. It reads a little oddly at first, then your inner voice reads
   it aloud properly at the end. The glossary carries your words into later messages.
5. **No dead ends** (the doc): nudges, hints, then after six misses the symbol solves itself.

The word matching is already built and tested (`SymbolMatcher`). Needed: the decoding screen, the
near-word lists, and the memory hints (data, one message at a time in `src/data/`).

## Open questions
1. The man in the outburst: a named local we'll see again, or anyone?
2. The ration share: at the hatch with a child from the lower roots (as drafted), or somewhere else?
3. At the fire: can you actually choose to burn it (an ignore path right away), or does the story
   always make you open it?
4. How much each public reaction adds to suspicion.
5. What the plate looks like: metal, bone, wood? (Grounded's markings are "in the tree's style but
   with a precision no tree carver could manage".)
