## Your work carries weight (designer)

The prince's duties aren't watching: **each day your father and your hand hand you work, and what you decide in it shows up later** (designer: your work should carry more weight). **(suggestion)** Each morning starts with the day's duties (the objective line lists them, in order); each duty has a small decision of its own, below the big and medium choices, whose result you see for yourself: below with your hand, in the halls, at the next audience, on the table of light. Small decisions are counted quietly (`lean`: people or order; `standing`: how upper society takes you), never shown, and colour the big choices (who argues with you in council, who comes to your broadcast).

## Every decision is clear (designer)

Chapter 2's decisions were confusing and not direct enough (designer). In Chapter 3, **every decision says plainly what you're choosing and what it means, at the moment you choose** (designer): a direct question, options worded as actions, and what's at stake said in the moment (who it touches, what happens next). Big and medium choices keep the second box that explains each option. Duty decisions are posed by whoever hands you the work, in a line, before the options ("Seal them, and the guard goes in tonight."). Hard consequences are fine (designer: signing the warrants for the raid you then walk into: "yes").

### The war table

**The simulation room** (designer): your father says "Come with me," and shows you a room where you practise the art of war. It's uneasy. **He's the opposition** (designer): a tutorial, but when he turns it on, he crushes you. The mechanic will come back in later chapters (designer). It needs a new area.

**What it could be modelled on** (research, 2026-10-14):

| Model | How it plays | For us | Against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Into the Breach** | A small grid; every enemy shows what it's about to do (an arrow, a target) before you move; each turn is a puzzle | Readable at our size; our fights already work on tells; deterministic, so his win feels earned | A grid of a field, not a city |
| **Advance Wars / Fire Emblem** | Turn-based armies on a map; unit types that counter each other; terrain; fog of war | Exactly "commanding an army"; well known | The heaviest to build (unit types, terrain, a clever AI) |
| **Risk / Polytopia / Slay** | Regions joined by borders; armies as numbers; take and hold ground | Very readable; scales to a whole city | Dry without something to watch |
| **Bad North / Tooth and Tail / Kingdom Rush** | Real time, small squads you point at places, waves | Pressure, spectacle | Hard to make an opponent that feels clever; heavier |
| **Diplomacy** | Everyone orders at once, then it resolves | Tension: you only see his move after you've committed | Needs several players to shine |

**Claude's recommendation (suggestion): the capital itself as the board.** A war table of the city, side-on like the map (it can reuse the map's painting: the citadel, the tiers, the low district, the lifts). Places are the squares; the halls, lifts and streets between them are the ways an army can move (Risk's regions, but the city's own). Turn-based, like our fights. You and he each move your squads one way per turn; where they meet, the stronger holds; attacking uphill is hard, and **the lifts** carry a squad several levels in one move. **Into the Breach's tells:** in the lesson, every one of his squads shows an arrow for where it'll go next, so you learn to read him.

- **The scenario is the unease:** "A district rises." He gives you the rising (the low district's people, many and weak) and takes the guard himself. He's practised putting down his own city.
- **The tutorial:** a few turns with his arrows showing; he lets you take the promenade. "Good."
- **You can only lose** (designer): there's no way to win the second half. **It's obvious when he turns it on** (designer) **(suggestion)**: he stands. The room's lights go down; the table's cyan turns hard white; the soft music cuts to a low pulse; he says one word ("Now."). His squads stop showing arrows, and he moves twice for every move of yours. Then: the arrows go (he stops telling you), he takes the lifts first so you can't climb, and he cuts the power in the districts you hold (your squads there can't move): the pushbacks you'll watch him make for real over the next week. Three turns, and it's over. He switches it off. A line, quiet, not cruel (designer to write); something like "I hope you never need it."
- **Drop yourself in** (designer): in a battle on the table, you can choose to drop yourself into certain fights, switching mechanics: the table hands over to a fight (our turn-based fights, the light-blade), and how it goes feeds back to the table (win, and your side holds or takes that place; lose, and it breaks). Then back to the table. **(suggestion)** The lesson on Day 2 teaches it: your father marks one fight "Go down yourself", and you do.
- **Your father is the face of the established order** (designer): like President Snow, the calm, civil face of the system the revolution is against, throughout, to Chapter 7 (which the designer is changing). On the table, the capital's side is always his, even when you're the one commanding it.
- **Later chapters** (designer): the same table, for real: Chapter 7, the revolution in the capital.
- **The new area:** the simulation room (designer: in your father's study; behind his bookshelf, suggestion): dark, the city in light across a room-sized table, the squads as points of light, screens round the walls. A scene of its own for the mechanic, like the fights.
