# Clipped Wing — Chapter 2: The Scavenger

Oct 4, 2026 · Harry Breeze-Lamb

Design for the second playable chapter. After Act 1 the game switches lead, so the tree's mystery stays intact. The wider cast and world notes live in [ideas.md](ideas.md).

## Pitch

A sister scavenges the dangerous, mafia-run ground at the tree's borderland to feed her little brother. She finds a dog in a world where dogs are basically extinct, and it sniffs out what nobody else can. Her luck gets her brought into the boss's inner circle, and then she's suspected of being the rat. Many people around her look guilty, but the boss who brought her in is the real rat, using her as an obvious scapegoat.

- **Arc:** nobody → insider → suspect → exile.
- **The dog** is both her rise and her downfall.
- **She has to leave** by the end whatever happens. Her last decision is one of power before she goes.
- **Design principle:** the choices determine the state of the society when you leave it.

## What the player knows

The player still doesn't learn the extent of the world or why it's like this. They only see that the finds are advanced, or from the old society. Nothing in this chapter explains the integrated or the tree.

## The world

- She lives at the borderland of the tree, in a scavenger society with lawless leaders.
- She scavenges to look after her brother. Their parents aren't around.
- The clans are technological and basic at the same time.
- **Finds are currency:** tax and food are both paid with what you find.
- The town is run like a mafia: families, turf, tribute, rations handed out as favours, and silence.
- The dog is an asset you can train to find things. You must keep it secret.

## Locations

The tree looms on the horizon from everywhere, the way the tube does in Act 1.

**The settlement**
- **Your shack:** home with your brother; the dog hidden under the floor.
- **The tally house:** where finds are weighed and taxed each evening. This chapter's version of Act 1's evening ceremony.
- **The swap yard:** the market, finds for food.
- **The bar/club:** where crews gather; gossip and suspicion spread here.
- **The gambling den.**
- **The brothel.**
- **The boss's compound:** off limits at first; opens once you're brought in.
- **The scrap stacks:** junk towers between the shacks, for climbing and hiding.

**The grounds**
- **The picked fields:** scavenged clean; where everyone works.
- **The sunk town:** half-buried ruins of the old society; rich but unstable.
- **The dog's hollow:** your secret training spot on the outskirts.
- **The rival line:** the border with another clan's turf.
- **The deep ground:** forbidden and far out.
- **The tree's edge:** where the borderland meets the tree's roots. Nobody goes close.

## Cast

**The boss's circle**
- **The boss:** charming and protective; secretly the rat.
- **The watcher:** quiet and intimidating, always at the boss's shoulder. Turns out fair and cunning: he has been hunting the rat himself, and he's the one you must convince in the end.
- **The enforcer:** loyal muscle who accuses you loudest. A red herring.
- **The boss's son:** your age, jealous of your rise.

**People you know** (friends, not a crew)
- **Your brother:** adores the dog and can't keep a secret.
- **Your friend:** lies about her nights because she's secretly seeing someone from the rival clan. A red herring.
- **The old scavenger:** gruff, doubts you, becomes something like a mentor.
- **The gambler:** a friend drowning in debt at the den who would sell you out. A red herring.
- **The kid lookout:** your brother's friend, who sees everything.
- **The girl who vanished:** the boss's last new recruit. Nobody talks about her.

**Around town:** the tallyman, the barkeep, the madam, the fence.

## The conflict

- **The overall conflict:** the rival clan is scavenging on your clan's turf.
- **The setup:** raids keep happening wherever and whenever you've been looking, so it looks like you're leading them in. The boss passes on the spots you report and the times of your watches.
- **Divided loyalties:** even though you never betrayed anyone, you can befriend the rival clan too.
- **The boss's motive:** years ago his daughter was handed to the rival clan to seal a truce, and the rival boss has held her as leverage ever since. Every secret he passes on keeps her alive.
- **The moral bind:** if you befriend the rival clan, you can learn this and be torn. Your new allies are holding a hostage, but the boss of your old clan set you up. Neither side is right.

## Chapter shape

Not day by day; the general flow.

1. **Scraping by.** A nobody on the picked fields, feeding your brother. Tribute night shows how the town runs. You find the dog *(medium choice: the dog)*.
2. **The rise.** The dog's finds make your hauls jump. The boss brings you into his circle and you meet the watcher, the enforcer and the son *(big choice 1: your brother)*. Then the first rival raid hits your turf (a story beat, not a choice).
3. **The squeeze.** Raids keep landing wherever you've been, and suspicion spreads from the bar to the compound. You chase red herrings *(medium choices: your friend's secret, the gambler's debt, the rival drop)*. You catch a rival scout *(big choice 2: the rival line)*.
   - **The reveal** lands at the end of the squeeze, before the final choice: the boss framed you.
     - Always: the dog recognises the scent from the rival drop on the boss. Nobody else in town could have this clue.
     - If you kept your brother with you: he saw the boss meet the rivals and tells you before he's caught.
     - If you befriended the rivals: they tell you who their contact is, and why (his daughter).
     - If you earned the watcher's respect: he reached the same suspicion, and you put it together.
   - Everyone learns it's the boss. How much you know, and who believes you, depends on your choices, which decides which endings are open.
4. **The fall.** The frame closes. You're branded and have to leave, without your brother *(big choice 3: power before you leave)*.

## Big decisions

### 1. Your brother (early)

Once the dog's finds get you brought into the boss's circle: do you move your brother into the boss's compound?

- **Yes:** he's safe and fed, but the boss brainwashes him. Eager to impress his new hero, he tells the boss about the dog, closing the frame on you. He stays with the boss when you leave.
- **No:** he helps you as a witness. Running with the kid lookout, he sees the boss meet the rival clan. But he gets caught and hurt, and you have to leave without him.

Either way, you leave without him.

### 2. The rival line (middle)

You catch a rival scout on your turf.

- **Turn them in to the watcher:** his respect, and evidence against the rivals.
- **Let them go and befriend the rival clan:** opens the path to the boss's daughter.

### 3. Power before you leave (ending)

You're branded and must go. Before you do:

- **Save the girl:** get the boss's daughter out. Frees him from the leverage, but leaves both clans angry. Needs the rivals' trust.
- **End the conflict:** expose the boss to the watcher and broker a truce. It costs you the dog's secret: to seal the truce you reveal the dog to both clans, so everyone now knows it exists and wants it. You keep the dog (she always leaves with it). Needs the watcher and the rivals.
- **Keep it raging:** expose the boss, let the clans go to war, and slip away in the chaos. Available to anyone.

## Medium decisions

- **The dog:** keep it, sell it or eat it. Anything other than keeping it gets turned back (your brother won't let it go, or it escapes and finds you again), so you end up keeping it.
- **Your friend's secret:** you find out she's meeting someone from the rival clan. Report her and you earn trust on your own side (the watcher). Cover for her and you earn the rival clan's trust.
- **The gambler's debt:** he begs for finds to pay off the den. Help him and he gives you a weapon. Refuse and he's the one who sells you out.
- **The rival drop:** while scavenging, the dog finds a hidden drop: a note in scavenger signs giving only a time and a place. It tells you someone in your clan is talking to the rivals, but not who or why (the daughter stays hidden until you befriend the rival clan).
  - **Leave it:** come back and watch the pickup, but you only see a hooded figure. A clue, not an answer.
  - **Take it:** proof a traitor exists, but you're the one holding a rival note.
  - **Swap in a fake:** the next raid hits where you sent them, proving the note real, but whoever reads the drop knows someone interfered.
  - Each gives a half-truth and makes you look a bit guiltier.
